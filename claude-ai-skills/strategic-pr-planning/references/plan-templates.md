# PR Plan Templates & Examples

## Complete PR Plan Structure

Use this template as a comprehensive checklist when creating full strategic PR plans.

### Executive Summary
- Organization overview
- Current situation analysis
- Key challenges identified
- Primary objectives
- Resource requirements

### Environmental Analysis
**SWOT Framework**:
```
Strengths:
- [Internal advantage 1]
- [Internal advantage 2]
- [Internal advantage 3]

Weaknesses:
- [Internal limitation 1]
- [Internal limitation 2]
- [Internal limitation 3]

Opportunities:
- [External possibility 1]
- [External possibility 2]
- [External possibility 3]

Threats:
- [External risk 1]
- [External risk 2]
- [External risk 3]
```

### Stakeholder Matrix Template

| Audience | Type | Priority | Key Interests | Communication Channel | Message Focus |
|----------|------|----------|---------------|----------------------|---------------|
| [Group 1] | Internal | High | [Interests] | [Channel] | [Focus] |
| [Group 2] | External | Medium | [Interests] | [Channel] | [Focus] |

### Image Assessment Survey Template

**Dimension Selection**: Choose 2-3 critical dimensions from:
- Trust/Credibility
- Awareness/Recognition
- Competence/Expertise
- Relevance/Timeliness
- Accessibility/Responsiveness
- Innovation/Progressiveness

**Survey Questions**:
1. How familiar are you with [Organization]? (Scale 1-5)
2. To what extent do you trust [Organization] to [primary mission]? (Scale 1-5)
3. How would you rate [Organization]'s ability to address [key issue]? (Scale 1-5)
4. What words come to mind when you think of [Organization]? (Open-ended)
5. Have you participated in any [Organization] activities? (Yes/No/Unsure)

### Strategic Goals Framework

**Problem → Strategy → Tactics**:

| Problem | Strategy | Tactics | Timeline | Responsible | Budget |
|---------|----------|---------|----------|-------------|--------|
| [Issue 1] | [Approach] | [Action 1], [Action 2] | [Duration] | [Person] | [Amount] |

### Crisis Communication Template

**Crisis Scenario**: [Type of crisis]

**Response Team**:
- Lead spokesperson: [Name/Role]
- Support team: [Names/Roles]
- Technical support: [Names/Roles]

**Holding Statement** (prepared in advance):
"[Organization] is aware of [situation]. We are currently [investigating/addressing] the matter and will provide updates as information becomes available. [Organization]'s priority remains [core value]. For immediate inquiries, contact [name] at [contact]."

**Communication Protocol**:
1. Assess situation (within [timeframe])
2. Notify crisis team (within [timeframe])
3. Prepare initial statement (within [timeframe])
4. Inform internal stakeholders (within [timeframe])
5. Release public statement (within [timeframe])
6. Monitor response and adjust (ongoing)

### Media Relations Toolkit

**Press Release Format**:
```
[ORGANIZATION LETTERHEAD]
[LOGO]

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
Contact: [Name]
Phone: [Number]
Email: [Address]
Date: [Date]

[HEADLINE IN TITLE CASE]

[Subheadline if needed]

[CITY, STATE] – [Opening paragraph with who, what, when, where, why]

[Body paragraphs with details, quotes, context]

[Boilerplate about organization]

###

[End of release indicator]
```

**Media Pitch Template**:
```
Subject: [Compelling, specific subject line]

Dear [Journalist Name],

[Hook - why this is newsworthy/relevant to their beat]

[Key information - 2-3 sentences]

[What you're offering - interview, data, expert commentary]

[Organization credibility - brief]

[Call to action - availability, contact info]

Best regards,
[Name]
[Title]
[Contact]
```

### Event Planning Checklist

**8-12 weeks before**:
- Define event purpose and goals
- Set budget
- Secure date and venue
- Identify key participants/speakers
- Begin guest list

**6-8 weeks before**:
- Design invitations
- Finalize program
- Arrange technical requirements
- Secure vendors (catering, AV, etc.)
- Create promotional materials

**4-6 weeks before**:
- Send invitations
- Begin promotion campaign
- Confirm speakers/participants
- Arrange signage and materials
- Plan logistics (parking, seating, etc.)

**2-4 weeks before**:
- Follow up with invitees
- Finalize attendance numbers
- Prepare run-of-show
- Conduct site visit
- Brief team members on roles

**1 week before**:
- Send reminders
- Confirm all vendors
- Prepare registration materials
- Test all equipment
- Print final materials

**Day of**:
- Arrive early for setup
- Conduct final equipment checks
- Brief staff on last-minute changes
- Maintain guest registry
- Document event (photos, notes)

**After**:
- Send thank-you notes
- Compile feedback
- Calculate ROI
- Document lessons learned
- Share event coverage

### Evaluation Metrics Framework

**Awareness**:
- Survey recognition scores (baseline vs. current)
- Media mentions (quantity and sentiment)
- Social media reach and impressions
- Website traffic

**Engagement**:
- Event attendance rates
- Social media interactions (likes, shares, comments)
- Email open and click rates
- Volunteer/member sign-ups

**Relationships**:
- Stakeholder satisfaction scores
- Partnership agreements established
- Repeat attendance/participation rates
- Referral rates

**Reputation**:
- Net Promoter Score
- Media sentiment analysis
- Third-party endorsements
- Awards/recognition received

**Operational**:
- Average response time to inquiries
- Communication deliverables completed on schedule
- Budget variance (actual vs. planned)
- Crisis incidents handled successfully

## Example: Student Organization PR Plan

This abbreviated example demonstrates application to a student organization context.

**Organization**: Student Organization of Faculty of Political Sciences (SOFPN)

**Situation**: 18-month-old organization with inconsistent results, weak internal recognition, no external visibility, no PR strategy.

**SWOT Summary**:
- Strengths: Quality membership (GPA >8.0), faculty support, first organization on faculty
- Weaknesses: No long-term strategy, weak brand, minimal activities
- Opportunities: Large untapped student base, potential for expanded programming
- Threats: New competing organizations, negative perception of student activism

**Key Objectives**:
1. Increase student awareness of SOFPN from 30% to 70% within 12 months
2. Establish SOFPN as competent advocate for student academic interests
3. Organize minimum 3 public events per semester

**Primary Strategies**:
1. Rebrand with clear mission: "We care about all FPN students, we care about knowledge, we care about the future"
2. Implement proactive communication via website, social media, and campus presence
3. Shift focus from social events to academic quality initiatives

**Tactics** (selected examples):
- October: Welcome events for new students, distribute information materials
- November: Host specialized seminar on improving student standards (international speakers)
- February: Launch redesigned website and active social media presence
- March: Public forum on relevant academic topic (e.g., investigative journalism)
- April: Organize study visits to regional institutions
- May: Conduct image assessment survey
- July: Second major public forum, evaluate annual program

**Differentiated Messaging**:
- To students: "We represent YOUR interests in improving academic quality"
- To faculty: "We support your efforts to maintain high standards"
- To other student orgs: "We seek collaboration, not competition"
- To external partners: "We bring student perspective to important discussions"

**Success Metrics**:
- Survey recognition increase from 30% to 70%
- Minimum 6 events with 50+ attendees each
- Establish 3 formal partnerships with external organizations
- Achieve positive coverage in 2+ regional media outlets

This example shows how the framework scales down for smaller organizations while maintaining strategic rigor.
